# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Long Island residents in NY-03 who have never read a water quality report: parents of babies, older residents, renters and homeowners. They arrive with one worry ("is my tap water OK?"), usually on a phone, often after a news story or a neighbor's comment. They want an answer in plain words, then the proof.

## Product Purpose
TapTrack puts each water district's yearly report side by side so a resident can see what is in their water, how it has changed, what the limits are, and what the district is doing about it. It is free, has no account, no ads and no affiliate links. Every number links to the district's own PDF. Success: a resident understands their water in under a minute and trusts the answer because they can check it.

## Brand Personality
Calm, plain, trustworthy. Like a good public library or a weather app: steady, clear, never alarming, never selling. Speaks the way a helpful neighbor would. Built by a high school student, and honest about it.

## Anti-references
- H2OScore and similar paid apps: locked results, a single made-up score, no sources.
- Filter-company "water report" pages: scary red warnings meant to sell a product.
- Government data portals: dense tables and jargon with no explanation.
- Emoji, gradient washes, hype.

## Design Principles
1. Plain words first, numbers second, sources always. Every number has a one-line meaning next to it and a link to its PDF.
2. Show, don't score. Compare a value to its limit visually instead of inventing a grade.
3. Calm, not alarming. Problems are stated clearly but never dramatized.
4. Explain on demand. Jargon (ppt, ND, 90th percentile, action level) is one tap away from a plain explanation, never required reading.
5. Not medical advice, on every screen with results.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA. Calibri (Carlito fallback) at 17px for body text, Arial for small labels; keep body text at least 4.5:1 contrast on white. Status never shown by color alone; always a word too. Large tap targets (48px). Full keyboard use. Reduced-motion alternatives for every animation. Interface text will be translated to Spanish, Chinese (Simplified) and Korean.
